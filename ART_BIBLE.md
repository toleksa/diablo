# ART_BIBLE.md
# Dark Fantasy Action RPG — Visual Direction & Asset Production Standards

**Document type:** Authoritative visual design specification  
**Purpose:** Establish and enforce consistent visual identity across all game assets.  
**Target genre:** Isometric 3D Action RPG / Dark Fantasy  
**Visual reference category:** High-production-value dark fantasy action RPGs  
**Status:** Active — mandatory for all visual asset production.

---

# 1. Core Art Direction

## 1.1. Vision

The game is a dark fantasy action RPG featuring an isometric camera, fast-paced combat, exploration, character progression, loot collection and elaborate environments.

The visual direction should communicate:

- A dangerous, ancient and mysterious fantasy world.
- A sense of scale and environmental storytelling.
- High-quality, detailed 3D environments.
- Distinctive character and enemy silhouettes.
- Powerful, visually satisfying combat abilities.
- Rich material definition and atmospheric lighting.
- Clear gameplay readability, even during visually intense encounters.

The overall presentation should feel like a professionally produced PC/console action RPG, not a mobile game, browser game or amateur indie prototype.

## 1.2. Visual Identity

**Primary aesthetic:**

- Dark Gothic Fantasy.
- Realistic stylization.
- Painterly environmental details combined with physically believable materials.
- Intricate architecture.
- Weathered stone, aged metal, leather, wood and organic materials.
- Dramatic environmental lighting.
- Controlled use of magical visual effects.

The world should feel physically believable, ancient and lived-in.

Avoid excessive photorealism. The target is a carefully art-directed fantasy world with a recognizable visual identity.

## 1.3. Visual Quality Target

Every major asset should meet the following standards:

- Convincing proportions and anatomy.
- Consistent perspective.
- Carefully designed silhouettes.
- Detailed surface materials.
- Intentional lighting and shadows.
- No visibly unfinished geometry.
- No generic AI-generated fantasy imagery.
- No inconsistent visual styles between assets.

The game should look cohesive when all assets are displayed together.

# 3. Environment Design

Environment art is one of the most important components of the game.

The world must feel handcrafted rather than assembled from generic modular assets.

## 3.1. Architectural Style

Use a combination of:

- Gothic architecture.
- Medieval European architecture.
- Ancient ruins.
- Massive stone fortifications.
- Religious structures.
- Underground crypts.
- Forgotten temples.
- Infernal architecture.

Architectural elements should have believable construction and structural logic.

Use:

- Tall stone arches.
- Massive columns.
- Ribbed vaults.
- Ornamental stonework.
- Broken masonry.
- Weathered statues.
- Iron gates.
- Wooden support structures.
- Decorative reliefs.

Avoid excessive ornamental detail on every surface. Important landmarks should receive more visual complexity than ordinary environmental objects.

## 3.2. Environmental Storytelling

Environments should communicate history through their visual composition.

Examples:

- Abandoned settlements show signs of hurried evacuation.
- Ancient temples reveal forgotten civilizations.
- Crypts contain recognizable burial architecture.
- Fortifications communicate military purpose.
- Infernal locations use unnatural geometry and hostile environmental features.

Every major environment should have a visual theme that distinguishes it from other regions.

## 3.3. Environment Categories

Maintain separate visual identities for:

### Wilderness

- Dead trees.
- Rocky terrain.
- Mud and dirt.
- Overgrown ruins.
- Sparse vegetation.
- Fog and atmospheric depth.

### Medieval Settlements

- Stone and timber buildings.
- Narrow streets.
- Market structures.
- Weathered rooftops.
- Decorative lanterns.
- Functional props and furniture.

### Catacombs and Crypts

- Repeating stone architecture.
- Burial chambers.
- Sarcophagi.
- Stone statues.
- Iron fixtures.
- Cold and subdued lighting.

### Ancient Ruins

- Monumental architecture.
- Broken columns.
- Ancient inscriptions.
- Weathered statues.
- Collapsed structures.
- Overgrown surfaces.

### Infernal Environments

- Dark stone.
- Unnatural architectural forms.
- Volcanic materials.
- Glowing fissures.
- Infernal structures.
- Strong directional lighting.

### Necropolis and Throne Halls

- Bone-white ash masonry and cold iron fittings.
- A single axis, read from a dais to a gate, dressed by a long blood-red carpet.
- Tall gothic furniture: a spined throne, a skull finial, banners hung flat behind it.
- Candlesticks as the light carriers rather than sconces, cold green flame.
- One square, enclosed room rather than a corridor web when the room is a stage.
- No portal anywhere in the last stage: the prize there is furniture, not a exit.

# 4. Character Design

Characters must be immediately recognizable from the isometric gameplay camera.

## 4.1. General Principles

- Strong and readable silhouettes.
- Believable anatomy.
- Distinctive equipment.
- Consistent proportions.
- Carefully designed armor.
- Recognizable class identity.
- High-quality material definition.

Characters should not look like generic fantasy NPCs.

Each major character should have a distinct visual identity.

## 4.2. Player Characters

Player characters should communicate their combat role through:

- Armor silhouette.
- Weapon type.
- Equipment.
- Animation language.
- Magical effects.
- Character proportions.

Avoid excessive visual clutter.

Equipment must remain readable at normal gameplay zoom.

## 4.3. Armor and Equipment

Armor should combine practical construction with fantasy aesthetics.

Material categories:

- Steel.
- Iron.
- Bronze.
- Leather.
- Cloth.
- Bone.
- Precious metals.
- Magical materials.

Materials must be visually distinguishable.

Metal should have appropriate specular highlights. Leather should appear relatively matte. Cloth should show believable folds and surface texture.

Avoid using identical material properties across all equipment.

## 4.4. Character Consistency

For every important character, maintain:

- Reference artwork.
- Front view.
- Side view.
- Back view.
- Color palette.
- Equipment reference.
- Silhouette reference.

When generating additional images of an existing character, use approved references.

Never redesign an established character unintentionally.

# 5. Enemy Design

Enemies should be visually distinct, readable and appropriate to their environment.

## 5.1. Enemy Archetypes

Maintain distinct visual categories:

- Undead.
- Humanoids.
- Beasts.
- Demonic creatures.
- Constructs.
- Magical entities.
- Elite enemies.
- Bosses.

Each category should have its own visual language.

## 5.2. Silhouette Design

Enemy silhouettes must communicate their archetype.

Examples:

- Large enemies: massive and imposing proportions.
- Fast enemies: narrow, dynamic silhouettes.
- Ranged enemies: recognizable weapons and posture.
- Armored enemies: heavy armor and strong shapes.
- Magical enemies: distinctive magical visual elements.

Do not rely exclusively on color to distinguish enemy types.

## 5.3. Elite and Boss Enemies

Elite enemies require more visual complexity than ordinary enemies.

Bosses should have:

- Unique silhouettes.
- Distinctive equipment or anatomy.
- Recognizable attack animations.
- Characteristic visual effects.
- Unique environmental presentation.

Bosses should be visually impressive without sacrificing gameplay readability.

The last overlord of the run, **Adrith the Lich King**, breaks the ladder on
purpose: his model is bred far wider than any overlord before him, he carries a
taller skull-crown than the crowned men wear, a cold aura under his feet and a
reaping scythe held out along his aim. His hands are legible at a glance: a
**grave chorus** that raises three of the fallen at his feet, a **cold grasp**
pulsing frost out of his ring and leaving the hero visibly chilled, and the
overlord's charge. His hall is read as an undead throne: the seat wakes only
after he is down, and it announces itself - cold flame, a pale word over its
head, a light carried through the dark - because a prize the player must walk
into has to be findable in the dark.

# 6. Weapons, Armor and Loot

Loot is a central visual component of the game.

Players must be able to distinguish equipment categories and rarity at a glance.

## 6.1. Weapon Categories

- Swords.
- Axes.
- Maces.
- Daggers.
- Spears.
- Bows.
- Crossbows.
- Staves.
- Magical implements.
- Class-specific weapons.

Each category must have recognizable proportions and construction.

## 6.2. Equipment Rarity

Use a consistent rarity hierarchy.

| Rarity | Visual identity |
|---|---|
| Common | Simple, functional materials |
| Magic | Subtle magical accents |
| Rare | More elaborate construction |
| Legendary | Distinctive silhouette and special details |
| Set | Shared visual identity across related pieces |

Rarity must not be communicated exclusively through excessive glow.

Legendary items should be interesting even when all magical effects are disabled.

## 6.3. Loot Presentation

Ground loot must be distinguishable from environmental props.

Important objects should have:

- Clear silhouettes.
- Appropriate contrast.
- Consistent presentation.
- Recognizable interaction indicators.

# 7. Combat Visual Effects

Combat effects must look powerful without obscuring gameplay.

## 7.1. General Principles

- Clear visual origin.
- Recognizable trajectory.
- Distinctive impact.
- Appropriate duration.
- Consistent color language.
- Controlled particle density.

Avoid excessive screen coverage.

The player must always be able to understand what is happening.

## 7.2. Elemental Effects

### Fire

- Warm orange and red palette.
- Dynamic flame shapes.
- Controlled sparks.
- Heat distortion where supported.

### Ice

- Cyan and pale blue.
- Sharp crystalline forms.
- Frost particles.
- Cold atmospheric accents.

### Lightning

- Bright, high-contrast electrical arcs.
- Short-lived flashes.
- Clear directional patterns.

### Arcane

- Blue and violet energy.
- Geometric magical patterns.
- Controlled luminous particles.

### Holy

- Warm gold and white.
- Radiant light.
- Elegant energy patterns.

### Poison

- Muted green.
- Organic particle shapes.
- Controlled atmospheric effects.

### Dark Magic

- Deep violet and black.
- Spectral particles.
- Unnatural light behavior.

## 7.3. Visual Readability

Never allow visual effects to permanently obscure:

- Player position.
- Enemy position.
- Enemy attack indicators.
- Important ground objects.
- Environmental hazards.

# 8. Lighting and Rendering

Lighting is essential to the atmosphere.

## 8.1. Lighting Philosophy

Use dramatic but readable lighting.

- Strong directional light.
- Controlled ambient illumination.
- Atmospheric fog.
- Localized light sources.
- Contact shadows.
- Subtle environmental reflections.

Avoid completely black shadows that hide gameplay information.

## 8.2. Material Definition

Materials must be physically distinguishable.

Recommended material characteristics:

| Material | Appearance |
|---|---|
| Stone | Rough, slightly varied |
| Metal | Reflective, directional highlights |
| Leather | Matte, subtle texture |
| Wood | Visible grain |
| Cloth | Soft surface, visible folds |
| Bone | Pale, slightly porous |
| Glass | Transparent or translucent |
| Magical materials | Controlled emission |

# 9. Camera and Composition

## 9.1. Gameplay Camera

The game uses an isometric or near-isometric perspective.

Visual assets must be designed for this perspective.

Prioritize:

- Top-facing surfaces.
- Side-facing geometry.
- Readable vertical structures.
- Clear object separation.
- Strong foreground/background contrast.

Avoid assets that only look good in a front-facing illustration.

## 9.2. Asset Composition

When generating environmental objects:

- Leave sufficient visual space around the subject.
- Avoid unnecessary background elements.
- Maintain consistent scale.
- Avoid extreme perspective distortion.

For isolated objects, use transparent backgrounds whenever possible.

# 10. User Interface

The UI should feel integrated into the world without sacrificing usability.

## 10.1. General Style

- Dark, restrained panels.
- Subtle metallic borders.
- Stone and aged material accents.
- Carefully controlled ornamental details.
- Readable typography.
- Consistent iconography.

Avoid generic flat mobile UI aesthetics.

Avoid excessive ornamentation that reduces usability.

## 10.2. UI Components

All components must share:

- Consistent corner treatment.
- Consistent border thickness.
- Consistent spacing.
- Consistent typography.
- Consistent icon proportions.
- Consistent hover and interaction states.

## 10.3. Typography

Use readable fantasy-inspired typography.

Headings may use a decorative serif typeface.

Body text, item descriptions and numerical information must prioritize legibility.

Do not generate text inside AI-generated images. All text must be rendered by the game engine.

## 10.4. Icons

Icons should be:

- Immediately recognizable.
- Consistent in visual weight.
- Designed for their actual display size.
- Readable against dark backgrounds.
- Consistent in perspective and shading.
- Drawn as one object centred on a square plate, with a frame that carries the
  state of the slot rather than the object's own colour.
- Composed the way the thing they stand for behaves, so the icon and the effect
  in the world are read as one thing and share its colours.

Avoid mixing flat vector icons with highly detailed painted icons.

The game's own skillbar is drawn, not painted: each of the eight class skills
carries its own inline-SVG plate, assembled at load from a handful of shared
primitives (a sweep band, a blade, a lance head, a lightning kink, an impact
star, a droplet) on a shared 40x40 viewBox, framed in currentColor so the rule
that dims an unusable slot dims the glyph too, and coloured with the exact
colours the cast itself throws on the floor. The set is meant to be read at the
slot's 56 px: one object, no lettering, no background, consistent weight.
Should painted icons be produced for the set (see prompts.txt), they replace
these plates one for one, keep each skill's existing silhouette and colour,
and the whole set is approved together — swapping one icon alone breaks the
consistency this section asks for.

# 11. AI Asset Generation Standards

This section is mandatory for any AI agent generating visual assets.

## 11.1. General Rule

Never generate an asset without first identifying:

1. Its intended purpose.
2. Its visual category.
3. Its target resolution.
4. Its required aspect ratio.
5. Its background requirements.
6. Its relationship to existing assets.
7. Its approved visual references.

Before generating an asset, consult this document.

## 11.2. Reference Images

Approved reference images are the primary source of visual consistency.

When generating related assets:

- Reuse approved references.
- Preserve established color palettes.
- Preserve established proportions.
- Preserve material characteristics.
- Preserve character identity.
- Avoid introducing new stylistic elements.

Do not treat unrelated AI-generated images as interchangeable references.

## 11.3. Prompt Construction

Every image generation prompt should contain:

1. Subject description.
2. Object or character category.
3. Visual style.
4. Material description.
5. Lighting description.
6. Composition.
7. Camera perspective.
8. Background requirements.
9. Technical output requirements.

Prompts should describe the actual intended asset, not merely its general mood.

### Example: Environment Concept Art

"An ancient abandoned Gothic stone fortress in a dark fantasy world, monumental medieval architecture, weathered limestone walls, intricate stone carvings, broken arches, subtle environmental storytelling, dramatic directional lighting, atmospheric volumetric fog, physically believable materials, carefully art-directed dark fantasy environment concept art, muted earthy color palette, high detail, professional game environment production quality."

### Example: Equipment

"A finely crafted medieval fantasy steel longsword, distinctive elegant silhouette, weathered forged steel, detailed crossguard, leather-wrapped handle, subtle ornamental engravings, physically believable materials, professional dark fantasy game equipment design, isolated object, orthographic three-quarter view, neutral background."

### Example: Character

"A heavily armored fantasy warrior, imposing but believable human proportions, intricate steel armor with aged leather components, distinctive readable silhouette, carefully designed equipment, realistic material definition, restrained dark fantasy aesthetic, professional AAA action RPG character concept art, neutral studio lighting, full body."

### Example: Game Icon

"A single detailed dark fantasy inventory icon representing an ancient magical amulet, centered composition, distinctive silhouette, aged metal and gemstone materials, controlled magical glow, consistent painterly game icon rendering, dark neutral background, no text, no lettering, square composition."

## 11.4. Negative Prompt

Use the following negative prompt as a general starting point, adapting it to the selected model:

"low quality, low resolution, amateur illustration, simplistic shapes, childish cartoon, flat mobile game graphics, generic fantasy stock art, inconsistent perspective, malformed anatomy, distorted proportions, inconsistent lighting, excessive bloom, oversaturated colors, noisy textures, muddy details, blurry surfaces, random decorative elements, text, watermark, logo, signature, duplicate objects, unwanted frame, collage."

Negative prompts are model-dependent. Do not assume that every model interprets them identically.

## 11.5. Image Generation Quality Control

Every generated asset must be reviewed for:

- Visual quality.
- Composition.
- Consistency with ART_BIBLE.
- Correct perspective.
- Correct proportions.
- Material definition.
- Background correctness.
- Technical usability.

Reject assets that are technically valid but visually inconsistent.

Generating an image successfully does not mean the asset is approved.

# 12. Asset Technical Specifications

These values are initial production standards. Adjust them to actual engine requirements, but never change them arbitrarily between related assets.

| Asset category | Target resolution | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Environment concept art | 1024–2048 px | PNG |
| Character concept art | 1024–2048 px | PNG |
| Inventory icons | 256–512 px | PNG/WebP |
| UI icons | 128–256 px | PNG/SVG |
| Portraits | 512–1024 px | PNG |
| Texture maps | 512–2048 px | PNG/WebP |
| UI backgrounds | 1024–2048 px | PNG/WebP |

## 12.1. Transparency

Use alpha transparency for:

- Isolated inventory objects.
- Character portraits when required.
- UI decorations.
- Particle sprites.
- Individual environmental props.

Do not assume that an image model produces a valid alpha channel.

When necessary, use a separate background removal or masking workflow.

## 12.2. Naming Convention

Use descriptive, predictable filenames.

Format:

`category_subject_variant_version.ext`

Examples:

- `weapon_sword_iron_v01.png`
- `armor_chestplate_steel_v01.png`
- `icon_potion_health_v01.png`
- `environment_crypt_wall_v01.png`
- `character_warrior_portrait_v01.png`
- `vfx_spell_fireball_v01.png`

Do not overwrite approved assets without explicit approval.

## 12.3. Asset Manifest

Maintain an asset manifest containing:

- Asset ID.
- Filename.
- Category.
- Description.
- Generation model.
- Workflow identifier.
- Seed, when available.
- Reference images.
- Resolution.
- Approval status.
- Intended game usage.

# 13. Asset Production Workflow

All visual production must follow this workflow.

1. Identify the missing asset.
2. Determine its role in the game.
3. Check whether a suitable asset already exists.
4. Consult ART_BIBLE.
5. Identify applicable reference images.
6. Prepare a detailed generation prompt.
7. Generate multiple candidates when appropriate.
8. Review candidates.
9. Approve or reject.
10. Export the selected asset.
11. Validate technical specifications.
12. Integrate into the game.
13. Verify appearance in actual gameplay.

## 13.1. Approval States

Use the following states:

- `planned` — asset identified, not generated.
- `generated` — candidate generated.
- `review` — awaiting visual approval.
- `approved` — accepted for production.
- `integrated` — integrated into the game.
- `rejected` — unsuitable for production.

An AI agent must never automatically promote an asset to `approved` based solely on successful generation.

## 13.2. Existing Asset Protection

Before replacing an asset:

1. Check its current usage.
2. Check whether it is already approved.
3. Preserve the previous version.
4. Generate a replacement separately.
5. Request approval before switching production references.

---

# 14. Strict Rules for AI Coding Agents

These rules are mandatory.

1. ALWAYS read ART_BIBLE.md before generating or modifying visual assets.
2. NEVER invent a new visual style for individual assets.
3. NEVER use primitive placeholder graphics as final production assets.
4. NEVER substitute unrelated stock images for missing game assets.
5. NEVER generate text, UI labels or numerical values inside images.
6. NEVER assume an image has correct dimensions or transparency without verification.
7. ALWAYS reuse established character and environment references.
8. ALWAYS maintain consistent naming conventions.
9. ALWAYS validate generated assets before integration.
10. NEVER overwrite approved assets without approval.
11. ALWAYS prioritize visual quality over generation speed.
12. ALWAYS verify assets in the actual game camera and gameplay context.
13. When no suitable asset can be generated, report the limitation instead of silently introducing a low-quality substitute.
14. If a new visual category is required, propose an extension to this document before establishing a new style.
15. Do not use copyrighted game assets, extracted textures, logos, character designs or copied UI elements as generated output.

---

# 15. Final Quality Standard

The ultimate objective is a visually cohesive, professionally presented dark fantasy action RPG.

A successful asset must satisfy three conditions:

**Artistic quality:** It looks professionally designed and rendered.

**Visual consistency:** It belongs naturally to the established world.

**Gameplay usability:** It works at the actual camera distance and does not interfere with gameplay readability.

When these requirements conflict, prioritize gameplay readability first, visual consistency second and decorative complexity third.

The game must never look like a collection of unrelated AI-generated images.

**ART_BIBLE.md is the authoritative source for all visual production decisions.**
